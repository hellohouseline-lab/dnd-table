# Campaign template

The shape a campaign takes so it can go straight into the table's story mode. Write your ideas however they come (a "big splurge"), then ask an AI to fit them into this template using the prompt at the end. Paste the result into **Paste your notes** on the Story tab (S1d), then add pictures, music and maps from your library.

Made for a normal party of 3 or 4 players and a DM. Written 27 October 2026, after studying how a published one-shot is put together (a two-person adventure, so its companion and romance parts were left out). Nothing from that book is copied here; the example is made up.

## How a good adventure is built

What makes a one-shot or a chapter run smoothly at the table, and what this template asks for:

- **One hook, one clock.** A strange event pulls the party in, and a deadline or pressure (the spring tide, a rival getting there first) keeps them moving. Every scene moves towards it.
- **Acts with a job each.** Setting up and getting the party together, the journey and the clues, then the reveal and a hard choice or a big fight.
- **People with handles.** Each person has a look, a voice and a manner the DM can act out, what they want, what they know and what they hide. The DM can play anyone from a few lines.
- **Something for everyone.** Each scene gives more than one kind of player something to do: someone to talk to, something to find, something to fight or sneak past.
- **Choices that change scenes, not the whole book.** What the party did earlier (whether they have the key, who they angered) changes how a later scene plays, written as a line or two, so the adventure stays writable.
- **Rolls with a purpose.** Every check has a DC, says what it's for, and says what happens on a fail (usually: it still happens, but it costs something).
- **Clues pointing at the truth.** Each place hides something that hints at the secret, and the reveal makes them all click.
- **Several endings.** The last scene allows a few outcomes depending on what the party chose, each with its own closing words.
- **Extras that set the mood.** Handouts, a map for each place where a fight might happen, music for each act, and a portrait for every important person.

## The template

Fill in what you know. Leave a line out rather than guess. Words in `<angle brackets>` are yours to replace. Lines marked *later* aren't held by the table yet (see "What the table holds" below) but are worth writing down now.

```markdown
# Campaign: <title>
Pitch: <one sentence: who, where, what's at stake>
Premise: <3 to 5 sentences the DM needs: what's really going on>
Truth: <the secret behind it all, which the players uncover>
Hook: <how it starts for the players>
Clock: <the deadline or pressure, if there is one>
Level: <starting level, and how many players>
Length: <expected hours>
Tone: <for example: cosy mystery, grim, romantic, funny>
Content notes: <themes to check with the group first>

## Cast

### NPC: <name>
Role: <innkeeper, captain, villain>

Wants: <what they're after>
Knows: <what they can tell the players>
Secrets: <what they hide>

Quest: <what they need help with, and the scene it happens in>
Fights as: <a monster from the free rules, such as "bandit captain", or a name from Monsters below>
Portrait: <file name in your library>

## Monsters
Only for anything not in the free rules. One per monster.

### Monster: <name>

## Items

## Flags
Things the story remembers, set in one scene and checked in another.
- <has the key>: set in <scene>, changes <scene and how>

## Places
- Picture: <file name>, for <scene>
- Map: <file name>, for <scene>, <grid size, if it has one>
- Music: <mood or track>, for <chapter or scene>

# Chapter 1: <title>

## Scene 1.1: <title>
Place: <where>
Picture: <file name>
Music: <mood or track>
Read aloud:
> <what the players hear on arrival: 60 to 150 words, present tense, ending on a question such as "What do you do?">
Reminders:
- <what must happen here>
- <what they might miss, and how to nudge them>
- <how the people here act>
People: <names from the Cast>
Rolls:
- <Perception> DC <13>, <secret DC>, <outcome shown>: <what it's for>. Pass: <...>. Fail: <...>.
Choices:
- <If they go to the docks>: Scene 1.2
Things:
- Item: <name>, <how they get it>
- Box: <chest, sack, body, desk or hoard>, <junk, ordinary, valuable or treasure>
- Shop: <name>
Fight: <2 bandit>, <1 Thug>; map: <file name>; ends when <someone flees or drops to half>
Sets: <flag>
If: *later* <flag or earlier choice>: <how the scene changes, in a line or two>

# Endings
## Ending: <name>
When: <what the players do to reach it>
What happens: <for the DM, a few lines>
Read aloud:
> <the closing words>
```

### Rules of thumb

- **How hard:** DC 10 easy, 15 medium, 20 hard (25 very hard). A level 1 to 3 character succeeds on DC 10 most of the time and DC 15 about half the time.
- **Failing forward:** say what a fail costs (time, hit points, a clue missed, someone's trust) rather than "nothing happens", unless nothing happening is fine.
- **Read-aloud** is short, in the present tense, speaks to the players as "you", and never says what they feel or decide.
- **Every scene has a way out:** at least one choice, or a line in Reminders saying what moves them on.
- **Three clues per secret:** anything the players must find out should turn up in at least three places.
- **One scene, one place.** A new place is a new scene, so **Go here** can change the picture and music.

## What the table holds today

| In the template | In the table | How it's added |
|---|---|---|
| Chapters and scenes, Place, Read aloud, Reminders | Story tab (S1a) | Paste your notes (S1d), or typed in |
| Picture, Music, Ambience | Scene's picture and sound | Picked from your library, a click each |
| Rolls (check, DC, secret, outcome shown, what for) | Planned rolls (S1b) | Added on the scene |
| Choices | Choices that jump to a scene (S1b) | Added on the scene |
| Things: items, boxes, shops | Things here (S1b), your items, shops | Added on the scene |
| Fight with free-rules monsters and a map | A fight ready to start (S1b) | Added on the scene |
| Cast: role, where, voice, wants, knows, secrets, attitude, portrait | NPCs tab (S1c) | Added on the NPCs tab |
| People in a scene | People in scenes (S1c) | Added on the scene |
| Pass and Fail results, Sets, Truth, Clock, Ends when | Reminders (as text) | Kept as words for the DM |
| Monsters not in the free rules | *Not yet*: only free-rules monsters | Use the closest free-rules monster for now |
| If (a scene changing by an earlier choice or flag) | *Not yet* | Kept in Reminders for now |
| Flags | *Not yet* | Kept in Reminders and the notebook |
| Handouts | *Not yet* (planned for later) | Show it as a picture on stage |
| Endings | Scenes in a last chapter | Each ending as a scene |

## The prompt for the AI

Paste this into a chat (Claude.ai is fine), followed by your notes. Ask it to keep going in rounds: it fills in what it can, lists what's missing as questions, and you answer.

```text
You're helping me prepare a Dungeons & Dragons (5th edition, 2014 rules) adventure for my online table.
I'll give you my notes, however rough. Turn them into the campaign template below, exactly as laid out:
the same headings, the same labels, one label per line, so my table can import it.

Rules:
- British English, plain words. No em dashes.
- Don't invent the big things (the secret, the ending, who the villain is). If I haven't said, ask.
  Small things (a look, a voice, a DC) you may suggest, marked "(suggested)".
- Read-aloud: 60 to 150 words, present tense, "you", ending on a question. Never say what the players feel or choose.
- Every roll has a check, a DC (10 easy, 15 medium, 20 hard), what it's for, and what happens on a pass and a fail.
- Every scene has a way out: a choice, or a reminder of what moves them on.
- Monsters: use free-rules (SRD) monsters by name where one fits; otherwise write a short stat block under Monsters.
- Anything the players must discover appears in at least three places.
- After the template, list what's missing or unclear as numbered questions, most important first.

<paste the template from "The template" above here>

My notes:
<paste your notes here>
```

## A short made-up example

```markdown
# Campaign: The Drowned Bell
Pitch: A fishing village hears a bell under the sea each night, and boats are vanishing.
Truth: The old bell-keeper rings a sunken bell to call a sea hag, who pays him in pearls.
Hook: A child gives the party a wet note: "The bell wants you next."
Clock: The spring tide in two nights, when the hag can walk on land.
Level: 2, for 3 or 4 players
Length: 3 hours

## Cast
### NPC: Old Tobin
Role: Bell-keeper
Where: The chapel on the cliff
Look: Stooped, salt-white beard, a pearl on a string at his throat
Voice: Slow, kind, calls everyone "little fish"
Wants: To keep his pearls coming
Knows: Where the sunken bell lies
Secrets: He rings it himself, every night
Attitude: Friendly
Fights as: commoner

# Chapter 1: The Village
Goal: The party learns about the bell and suspects the chapel.
Ends when: They climb to the chapel.

## Scene 1.1: The Gull and Anchor
Place: The village inn
Read aloud:
> Rain drums on the shutters of the Gull and Anchor. Half the tables are empty, and the fishermen who are here talk in low voices. Then, faint and far beneath the sound of the sea, a bell rings. Every head turns towards the window. Someone drops a cup. What do you do?
Reminders:
- The innkeeper won't talk about the bell until the fishermen leave.
- A wet trail of pearls leads from the back door to the cliff path.
People: Old Tobin
Rolls:
- Perception DC 13, secret DC: to spot the pearls by the back door. Pass: they find the trail. Fail: Tobin pockets them first.
Choices:
- If they follow the trail: Scene 1.2
```
